Weiping Li, Matthew Fang, Claire Chen, Naga Chintalapati, Efthimios Gkatzimas, Rhea Haridas, Aditya Kaul, Ashita Pant, Vunal Jinasundera

## Abstract

Hall thrusters (HTs) are the most widely used electric propulsion systems in development today. However, accurate modeling remains challenging due to problem stiffness, complex multiphysics interactions, and poorly understood particle-level phenomena inherent in HT plasmas. For the past two decades, the standard has been to prioritize computational efficiency by assuming a quasi-1D electron fluid and quasi-neutral electric field. In this work, we review the assumptions behind this standard model, examine the computational trade-offs of reintroducing more complete physics, and present early qualitative successes from a 2D hybrid-PIC simulation incorporating full Euler fluid dynamics, a Poisson solver, and DSMC based fluid-to-particle collisions.
